UE5 · Portal 2 · BattleBlock

Three levels from one level design class, each in a different genre and tool. There’s a shooter level in Unreal Engine 5, a laser puzzle in Portal 2’s Puzzle Maker, and a platformer level in BattleBlock Theater’s level editor.

I designed and built each level on my own. Every one started as a written plan and a layout drawing, then went through a build, playtesting and a revision, with the changes logged in a postmortem.

  • FPS level — close quarters and vertical layers, with two paths
  • Portal 2 puzzle — a three-room lesson in lasers and redirection cubes
  • BattleBlock level — a platformer that starts with a leap of faith off a cliff

FPS Level

This single-player shooter level in Unreal Engine 5 is built around close quarters, choke points and vertical layers. Each of its four rooms is layered differently, so you have to stay aware of enemies above and below you. From the first room you can take the stairs up or a tight tunnel underneath.

Playtest changes

  • Nearly every tester took the stairs and skipped the tunnel. I moved the weapons chest to the end of the tunnel to draw players in, and added a chest on the stairs path so both routes reward you.
  • A long ladder up to the arena slowed the pace, so I swapped it for a bounce pad, as one tester suggested.
  • The final room was far too simple, with two enemies wandering on two layers. I expanded the lower floor and added more enemies and cover for a real final fight.
  • Most testers finished at very low health, so I added health packs through the level.
  • One tester walked back to the start without realizing it, so I added yellow lights on paths that lead back and green lights on the way forward.

Portal 2 Puzzle

This three-room puzzle level teaches lasers and redirection cubes. The first room is a low-risk place to learn the cube, the second uses portals to bend the beam, and the last pulls both together.

Playtest changes

  • The opening lasers were a bit misleading and didn’t teach much, so I added laser fields behind them that switch off when you reflect a beam away with the cube, as an early lesson in using it.
  • The second room drew the most complaints. I shortened the hall, made the portal surface on the ceiling easier to line up with the catcher, and replaced the water under the moving platform with a lift, so falling no longer meant starting over.
  • The third room was the best received and barely changed. I made its back wall non-portalable so players couldn’t skip the puzzle.

BattleBlock Theater Level

“The Cliff” is a platformer level about exploring with very little information. You take a leap of faith off a cliff, then climb back up through water, spikes and lasers that get harder as you go, ending in a fight against ranged enemies at the top.

Playtest changes

  • The ice jumps were the hardest part of a timed level, so I widened one tier of the ice platforms and added recovery holes under the pillars.
  • Players used an enemy’s head as a step to skip a climb, so I moved the enemy into the wall.
  • Most testers wanted a checkpoint after the big water pit. I didn’t think the jump was that hard, but enough players struggled that I added one anyway.
  • The final climb was too easy, so I added a ranged enemy at the top that shoots down while you wait on the ladder.

Takeaways

  • The first two levels I made, BattleBlock and Portal 2, were hard for the sake of being hard. That works if the game is built around punishing challenge, but for the average player it caused more frustration than satisfying difficulty. A lot of the playtest fixes gave players a way to recover from a mistake, like recovery holes, a lift in place of deadly water and an extra checkpoint.
  • In the FPS level, most players missed the height advantages because they were focused on the enemy right in front of them. A sign pointing up as they enter a room might help them find those on their own.
  • In Portal 2, the rooms that built on what players already knew went over best. The third room brought the lasers and portals from the earlier rooms together and was the best received. The second room’s out-of-the-box portal trick was hard to pick up on, and it drew the most complaints.
